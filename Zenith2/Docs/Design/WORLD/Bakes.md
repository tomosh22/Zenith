# WORLD design: baking workflows

Section file of `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD.md`. It specifies WORLD's three baking workflows — lighting, navigation and HLOD — and the orchestration they share: bake units, input fingerprints, the bake planner and its ordering, execution, cancellation, resumption and the bake report. WORLD owns the workflows; each output's format and its cook step are the owner's: LIGHT's lightmap, irradiance volume and probe formats (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/GI.md` → "Bake output contract"), AI's navigation tiles (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/AI/Navigation.md` → "Bake inputs"), GEOM's meshes (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/GEOM/Meshes.md` → "Mesh cook pipeline"). LIGHT assigns the lightmap tracer, probe placement and bake scheduling to WORLD (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT.md` → "Purpose and scope"), so the tracer is specified here.

## Workflow overview

| Workflow | Explicit or in the cook | Unit | Output | Who cooks the output | Freshness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lighting | explicit: `world.bake.lighting` in the editor or an automation script | one cell of the lighting grid | the cell's light bake header and page documents (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Documents.md` → "Baked lighting documents"), tracked in source control | the importer `world.world` turns them into LIGHT and GEOM intermediates; LIGHT's and GEOM's steps cook them | the unit's fingerprint against the header's recorded one |
| navigation | in the cook | one navigation region | the region's navigation input scene and AI's products, declared by the importer (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Partition.md` → "Navigation input scenes") | AI's `ai.navMesh` | always fresh after a cook: ASSET's keys re-bake exactly the tiles whose inputs changed |
| HLOD | in the cook | one HLOD node | the node's mesh merge recipes (`hlod:` products) | GEOM's `geom.mesh` | always fresh after a cook: a recipe is a pure function of the cell content |
| generators (baked mode) | explicit: `world.generate` | one region job | generated region documents | the partition | `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Generation.md` → "Generator outputs and staleness" |

<!-- elaboration -->
Lighting is the one explicit bake because its cost (minutes per cell on the reference desktop) would otherwise run inside every developer's cook after any edit within the influence radius; explicit baking keeps the cook's no-op time within Z2-B-ASSET-011 and keeps a moved lamp from blocking play-in-editor, at the price of a staleness signal, which every cook reports (W41) and PACK's release check refuses (row WD14 (b)). Navigation and HLOD are cheap per unit and must never disagree with the geometry they describe (a stale navmesh walks agents through a new wall; a stale proxy pops), so they ride ASSET's incremental keys.

## Input fingerprints

A fingerprint is a SHA-256 (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/CORE/Maths.md` → "Zenith_Sha256") over a byte sequence the unit kind defines, built with `Zenith_WorldFingerprintBuilder` (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/PublicAPI.md` → "Fingerprint builder"), whose members append little-endian fixed-width values (`AddU32`, `AddU64`, `AddF32Bits`, `AddF64Bits`, `AddID` as the 16-byte cooked form, `AddDigest` as 32 bytes, `AddText` and `AddBytes` as a `uint32` byte length then the bytes, `AddHash128` as low then high word). Fingerprints are persisted in documents, so they use SHA-256 and never `Zenith_Hash64`, whose algorithm D-SEL-HASH (open, Stage 1) may change.

### Lighting fingerprint

A lighting unit's fingerprint F has two levels, so that the geometry part can be recomputed from the geometry the tracer actually decoded ("Bake execution"). The **input digest** P is the SHA-256 of items 1 to 7 below, in this order, for lighting unit U of cell (g, x, z) with rectangle Rc and gather rectangle Rg = Rc grown by `influenceRadius` on each side; the **mesh list** M is the ascending list of distinct mesh GUIDs item 4 names, each with the LOD 0 geometry digest of its summary ("Mesh summaries" below); then

`F = SHA-256( AddText("ZWLIGHT2"), AddDigest(P), AddU32(|M|), for each (mesh, digest) of M: AddID(mesh), AddDigest(digest) )`

computed by `Zenith_FinishWorldLightingFingerprint` (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/PublicAPI.md` → "Bake planning functions").

1. `AddText("ZWLIGHT2")`, `AddU32(uWORLD_LIGHT_TRACER_VERSION)`, `AddU32(uWORLD_PROBE_FACE_SIZE)`.
2. The resolved lighting settings (`Zenith_WorldLightingSettings` after defaults are applied), member by member in the row order of "Lighting settings" (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Documents.md`): booleans and integers `AddU32`, numbers `AddF32Bits` (the reader narrows each document number once), colours and directions as three `AddF32Bits`, the grid as `AddText` of its name, `sun` as `AddU32(0)` when null else `AddU32(1)` and its members, `materialAlbedo` as `AddU32(count)` then, in ascending material GUID, `AddID` and three `AddF32Bits`. The settings' canonical bytes are not hashed, so a reformatted document, an unknown member or an explicit default changes nothing, and no member of the settings depends on the world's layers.
3. `AddText(grid name)`, `AddU32((uint32)x)`, `AddU32((uint32)z)`, `AddF64Bits` of Rc's minimum and maximum X and Z, `baseY`, `height`.
Every list below is preceded by its element count (`AddU32(count)`, written also when the count is 0), so the byte sequence is prefix-free: no two different inputs encode to one stream. A world rotation is written as the four `AddF32Bits` x, y, z, w of its `Zenith_Quaternion`, in that order, and a scale as the three `AddF32Bits` x, y, z.

4. `AddU32(count)`, then for every entity carrying `Zenith.Render.MeshInstance` in a hierarchy of a BAKED layer (whatever its eligibility) whose **world bounding sphere** ("Mesh summaries") intersects Rg (the distance from the sphere's centre to Rg's XZ rectangle, computed in `double`, is at most its radius), from the resolved content, sorted by entity ID: `AddID(entity)`, `AddID(mesh GUID)`, the eight material-override GUIDs (`AddID` each, null for none), the world translation as three `AddF64Bits`, the rotation and the scale as stated above, then the eligibility inputs `AddU32` each: its layer's `bakes.lighting`, property 15 `Visible`, 1 when the entity is static and 0 when it is moving ("Grid assignment" in `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Partition.md`), the hierarchy's assignment to the lighting grid, and `receiver` (1 when "Bake geometry" makes it a receiver). The mesh's geometry enters through M, not here.
5. `AddU32(count)`, then for every light component (`Zenith.Lighting.PointLight`, `.SpotLight`, `.DirectionalLight`) of a BAKED layer with `bakes.lighting` true or of the persistent scene, whose influence (range sphere; directional lights always) intersects Rg, sorted by entity ID: `AddID(entity)`, `AddText(stable name)`, `AddBytes(<the component's binary field-set record>)` (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/ECS/Reflect.md` → "Binary field-set record"), the world translation and rotation as in 4.
6. `AddU32(count)`, then for every baked reflection probe of the unit ("Bake geometry"), sorted by entity ID: `AddID`, translation as in 4, `AddBytes(<its field-set record>)`.
7. `AddU32(count)`, then for every terrain of the world's `terrains`, in ascending GUID, whose extent (`origin` plus `tiles × 128 m` on X and Z) intersects Rg: `AddID(terrain)`, `AddF64Bits` of each of `origin`'s three values, `AddF32Bits` of both `heightLimits`, `AddU32(count)` of `tileFiles` and its (x, z) coordinates as two `AddU32` each in (z, x) order (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/ENV/Bake.md` → "Terrain document schema"); then `AddU32(count)` of the listed tiles whose 128 m rectangle intersects Rg and, for each in (z, x) order, `AddU32(x)`, `AddU32(z)` and `AddDigest(SHA-256 of the tile document's bytes)`; then, once when the terrain count is not 0, `AddU32(uENV_NAVIGATION_GEOMETRY_VERSION)`, the version of ENV's `Zenith_ExtractNavigationGeometry` output (row WD26), because the bake's terrain triangles are that function's output. A terrain whose `origin` or `heightLimits` moves changes every unit it reaches, though no tile changed.

The tracer's random draws are not an input of any unit, because they depend on nothing a document holds: every draw is keyed by the constant seed `ulWORLD_LIGHT_TRACER_SEED`, the unit key (item 3's grid and coordinates) and the texel, probe, sample and depth ("Tracing", "Random draws"). The world document's `seed` is therefore not a lighting input, and changing it stales no unit except through the cook-mode generator outputs it moves, whose transforms item 4 hashes. `uWORLD_LIGHT_TRACER_VERSION` is bumped by any change to the tracer's output for unchanged inputs, and a change of `Zenith_Hash64`'s algorithm (the D-SEL-HASH ruling, or any later change of CORE's hash) is such a change, so the ticket that lands it bumps the constant in the same diff and every lighting unit becomes STALE (W41). The obligation "a lighting unit's documents are a pure function of its fingerprinted inputs and the tracer version" (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD.md` → "Determinism obligations") holds because every value the tracer reads is in items 1 to 7 or M: the settings (2), the cell (3), every occluder and receiver with its transform and eligibility (4) and its decoded geometry (M), every light (5), every probe (6) and the terrain (7).

The importer computes F at every import, for every unit whose header exists, from the documents and summaries it already holds, and compares it with the header's `fingerprint` (W41); the editor's planner computes it from the plan's pinned documents and summary table. Both call the same function `Zenith_ComputeWorldLightingFingerprint` over a `Zenith_WorldSourceAccess`, which reads only the access's documents, product lists and summaries and sorts every collection it hashes, so the result depends on the documents and summaries alone and never on which implementation of the access supplied them or in what order (`Test_WorldBake_FingerprintIndependentOfSourceOrder`).

#### Mesh summaries

A mesh's **LOD 0 summary** is GEOM's published property `geom.lod0Summary` (row WD30), 48 bytes little-endian: the 32-byte **LOD 0 geometry digest** (the SHA-256, computed by GEOM's `Zenith_ComputeMeshGeometryDigest`, over the decoded LOD 0 positions, normals and UV1 of the cooked Windows mesh, its indices, its per-triangle material slots and its slot material GUIDs, in the byte layout row WD30 fixes), then the object-space bounding sphere's centre as three `f32` and its radius as one `f32`. The sphere is computed from the same decoded LOD 0 positions by a fixed algorithm: the centre is the centre of their axis-aligned bounding box, computed in `double` and rounded to nearest `f32`; the radius is the largest distance from that `f32` centre to a position, computed in `double` and rounded up to the next `f32`. Both are therefore functions of the geometry the tracer reads and of nothing else: an `uZENITH_ENGINE_COOK_VERSION` bump, a codec or library upgrade, a compression level or a GEOM setting that leaves LOD 0's decoded values alone changes no summary and therefore stales no unit, while any change of the decoded triangles does.

An instance's **world bounding sphere** is the summary's centre transformed by the instance's world matrix (translation in `double`, rotation and scale in `float`, the result in `double`) and the summary's radius times the largest absolute component of the instance's world scale, in `double`. It is the one source of mesh bounds in WORLD: fingerprint item 4's membership, the occluder and receiver tests of "Bake geometry" and HLOD's `minInstanceRadius` ("Instance eligibility") all use it, so the importer and the editor, which read the same property, compute the same sets. The importer reads the summary with `ReadPublishedProperty(mesh, ASSET_PLATFORM_WINDOWS, "geom.lod0Summary")` (row WD31), recorded as an import input, for every mesh a `Zenith.Render.MeshInstance` of a BAKED layer names whenever lighting is enabled and any light bake header exists, or HLOD is enabled; the editor's plan operation reads it from the cook server's answer for the plan's `GetLightingMeshes` (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Editing.md` → "World editor extension"); both through `Zenith_WorldSourceAccess::GetMeshLod0Summary`.

## Bake planner

`Zenith_PlanWorldBakes(const Zenith_WorldBakePlanInput&, Zenith_Arena&, Zenith_WorldBakePlan&)` (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/PublicAPI.md` → "Bake planning functions") runs in the editor inside the plan operation's fingerprint job, over that operation's partition plan and source access (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Editing.md` → "World editor extension"):

1. Enumerate every unit of every kind ("Bake units") over the plan's documents, which are the world's saved documents with their digests.
2. Compute each explicit unit's fingerprint and state; give the in-cook kinds the state `IN_COOK` with the last cook's result for the unit's products, read from `Zenith_WorldBakePlanInput::m_axLastCookResults` (the editor's main thread copies the cook client's last results for the world's products into the plan operation when it starts the fingerprint job, an O(products) copy; the cook client itself is never called from a job).
3. Build the dependency graph: a generator unit precedes every navigation unit of its region and its neighbours, and every lighting unit whose gather rectangle intersects its region grown by the generator's displacement bound (`world.lattice` `√2 × jitter`, `world.spline` `jitter`, the others 0; `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Generation.md` → "Obstacles and dependencies"), since its outputs may stand that far outside the region that owns them; a lighting unit depends on nothing else; navigation and HLOD units run inside the cook after the explicit units' outputs are written.
4. Order: Kahn's algorithm over the graph, ties by (kind order generator, lighting, navigation, HLOD; then key byte order). The order is a pure function of the units, so two runs bake in the same order.
5. Select: a run bakes the STALE and MISSING units of the requested kinds and scope (an author's selection, the panel's "Bake stale", or every unit with `force`), plus every unit the selection depends on that is not FRESH; EDITED generator units are skipped unless `overwriteEdited`.

Complexity: O(U log U + E) for U units over E resolved entities; fingerprinting reads every input once.

## Bake execution

`Zenith_WorldBakeOperation` (`Zenith2/Tools/ZenithEditor/Internal/World/Zenith_WorldBakeOperation.h`, `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Editing.md` → "Bake panel and commands") is an editor **background operation**, not a history record: it holds an operation handle shown with its progress in the status bar (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/EDIT/Foundation.md` → "Editor frame": every operation longer than `editor.frame.syncBudgetUs` is a background operation), it is driven by `Poll` from the editor frame on the main thread, and it never locks the history, so the editor accepts edits, saves and Play while it runs. It bakes the selected units of one **pinned plan** (the plan operation's latest completed plan when the bake starts, kept alive until the bake ends), in plan order, one unit at a time. Its only main-thread work is bookkeeping: submitting jobs, polling handles, starting the cook request and the unit's `SaveDocuments` batch; gathering, packing, the BVH build, the tracing and the encoding all run in jobs. Every job is submitted with `Zenith_JobSystem::SubmitBackground` at the tools-host priority of [Z2-N-ARCH-443] (`JOB_PRIORITY_BACKGROUND_TOOL`, name `"bg/WorldBakeItem"`, the paragraph after the state table), at most `2 × GetMaxConcurrentToolBackground()` in flight; a submission refused with `JOBS_ERROR_QUEUE_FULL` stays pending and is retried at the next `Poll`. No job forks, joins or waits: each job processes one step or one work item and returns, and the operation observes completion through the operation handles.

| State | Event | Next | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| IDLE | `Start(pinned plan, selection)` | COOKING | refused, with nothing started, as `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Editing.md` → "World editor extension" ("Starting a pinned operation") states: a document the plan read is DIRTY, or a path of its read table changed after its `G0` (the panel offers "Save and bake"); otherwise request a `cook` from the cook server for every mesh the selected lighting units read, so the cooked files the tracer decodes exist |
| COOKING | the cook request completes | PREPARING | the per-unit generation check of the first unit, then submit one job running `Zenith_MakeWorldLightBakeJob` (the unit's snapshot from the pinned plan, terrain read through the operation's pinned access) then `Zenith_PrepareWorldLightingUnit` (gather, decode, pack, BVH build) |
| COOKING | the cook request fails | FAILED | nothing written |
| PREPARING | the prepare job completes | TRACING | submit the unit's work items |
| TRACING | `Poll` finds every work item of the unit complete | WRITING | submit one job running `Zenith_FinishWorldLightingUnit` then `Zenith_WriteWorldLightBakeDocuments`, which also opens the unit's existing header and every existing page document through the pinned access (the header against the plan's recorded digest; the pages, which the plan never opens, recorded at this first open); when it completes, issue one `SaveDocuments` batch of the unit's header and pages, each write with its expected old digest the file digest that job read (none for a new file), backed up, which also deletes every existing page document of the unit whose index is at least the new `pageCount` (each deletion with that digest); a page or header changed since that open fails the batch with `STALE_PLAN` |
| WRITING | `SaveDocuments` of the unit's batch succeeds | PREPARING (next unit) or DONE | the report gains the unit's row with the batch's backup ID; the unit's header and page paths are a plan trigger ("World editor extension" step 1); the next unit starts with its generation check; the unit is FRESH in the next plan unless an input was saved since the pinned plan or the decoded geometry differs from the published summary |
| PREPARING | the per-unit generation check finds a path of the plan's read table changed after `G0`, or the prepare job fails with `WORLD_ERROR_SOURCE_ACCESS` / `DOCUMENT_ERROR_STALE_PLAN` | PREPARING (next independent unit) | the unit is FAILED with "input changed during bake; bake again", nothing of it written; the check is O(paths read) map lookups on the main thread, so a stroke saved after `Start` and then undone and saved again still fails the unit |
| WRITING | `SaveDocuments` fails (`DOCUMENT_ERROR_STORAGE_LOCKED`, `LOW_DISK`, `STALE_PLAN`) | PREPARING (next independent unit) or DONE | the unit is FAILED and its outputs are discarded; earlier units stay written; dependents of the unit are skipped (the same rule as a unit failing in PREPARING or TRACING) |
| WRITING | the finish-and-write job fails (`WORLD_ERROR_OUT_OF_MEMORY`, `WORLD_ERROR_BAKE_CORRUPT` reading an existing page, `WORLD_ERROR_INVALID_DESC` from an item) | PREPARING (next independent unit) or DONE | the unit is FAILED with the error; nothing of it written |
| COOKING | cancel | CANCELLED | the cook request is cancelled through the cook client; nothing written |
| WRITING | cancel | CANCELLING | no further job is submitted; an in-flight finish-and-write job or `SaveDocuments` batch completes (the batch commits whole or fails whole, never partly); then CANCELLED, the units not started listed |
| any state with jobs | a submission is refused with `JOBS_ERROR_INVALID_DESC` (the job system did not opt in to `JOB_PRIORITY_BACKGROUND_TOOL`) or `JOBS_ERROR_SHUTTING_DOWN` | FAILED | the operation stops; nothing of the current unit is written; earlier units stay written; the refusal is in the report |
| any state with jobs | a submission is refused with `JOBS_ERROR_QUEUE_FULL` | unchanged | the submission stays pending and is retried at the next `Poll` |
| PREPARING, TRACING | cancel | CANCELLING | no further job is submitted; the operation sets the cancellation flag every item checks between its rows of texels and between probe faces, and the prepare job between its gather, decode, pack and build phases; nothing of the unit is written |
| CANCELLING | every submitted job of the unit has completed | CANCELLED | the report lists the cancelled unit and the units not started |
| PREPARING, TRACING | a unit fails (`WORLD_ERROR_ATLAS_FULL`, `WORLD_ERROR_BAKE_GEOMETRY`) | PREPARING (next independent unit) | the unit is FAILED in the report; independent units continue; dependents of a failed unit are skipped |
| DONE, CANCELLED, FAILED | — | IDLE | the pinned plan is released; the bake panel shows the report; the edit world's play copies reload the changed documents through the cook server |

[Z2-N-ARCH-443] (`Zenith2/Docs/Architecture/CPUFrameGraph.md`; ruling X350 (a), human may override) is the rule WORLD's editor jobs follow: in a tool host alone a background job at `JOB_PRIORITY_BACKGROUND_TOOL` may run past the 2 ms slice of [Z2-N-ARCH-393], at most `m_uMaxConcurrentToolBackground` workers (read through `GetMaxConcurrentToolBackground()`) run such jobs at once (default max(1, N / 2) for N workers), and `jobs.bg.slice_overruns` does not count them. Every WORLD job of the editor (the plan operation's, the bake's and `world.generate`'s) uses that priority and no other, so review checks them against [Z2-N-ARCH-443] rather than [Z2-N-ARCH-393].

JOBS declares the priority (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/JOBS/Algorithms.md` → "Tools-host background priority"; answer row J25): `JOB_PRIORITY_BACKGROUND_TOOL` (`Zenith_JobTypes.h`) is accepted by a job system created with `Zenith_JobSystemDesc::m_bAcceptToolBackground` true, which the editor's composition root (MOD's tool-mode `Zenith_RuntimeToolAccess::Create`) and the cook's set in code; a TOOL submission to any other job system returns `JOBS_ERROR_INVALID_DESC` with nothing issued, so every WORLD test host that runs an editor operation creates its `Zenith_JobSystem` with that field set (or boots through MOD's tool access). WORLD bounds its in-flight submissions per operation by `2 × Zenith_JobSystem::GetMaxConcurrentToolBackground()` (the resolved `m_uMaxConcurrentToolBackground`, `min(max(1, ⌊N / 2⌋), background cap)` by default). A tool job uses no `ParallelFor`, and each WORLD tool job's longest interval between cancellation checks is the "Job lengths" bullet below ([Z2-N-CONTRACT-338]).

- **Job lengths.** Under X350 no WORLD editor job re-submits itself in slices; each runs to its end or to its next cancellation check. The longest are a plan operation's partition (O(E log E) over the world), a prepare job's BVH build (O(T log T), T ≤ 16,777,216) and a 64 × 64 tile of a page (4,096 texels × `samplesPerTexel` × up to `bounces` path vertices plus one shadow ray per light at each), and a cancellation takes effect within one row of 64 texels of every running item.
- **Outside the history.** Bake documents are derived outputs, not edits: the operation records no history record and no fence, and its writes are editor-originated writes to documents that are not open (the inspector reads bake documents through the source access without opening them as editor documents, `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Editing.md` → "World editor extension"), so the rule "Editor-originated writes" of `Zenith2/Docs/Design/EDIT/Foundation.md` applies in its "not open" row. Undoing a bake is "Restore this batch" on the backup batch the report names for each unit (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/EDIT/Pipeline.md` → "Restoring a backup"), which restores the previous header, pages and every page the batch deleted, or a re-bake; undoing an edit made before or during a bake never needs the bake undone, because the edit, once saved, makes the unit STALE through its fingerprint.
- **Atomic units.** Each unit's outputs (a lighting header, its pages and the deletion of its surplus pages) are one `SaveDocuments` batch, journaled and backed up (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/SCENE/DocumentFramework.md` → "Journaled write"), each write with its expected old digest, so a unit is either wholly written or not at all, no page document of an earlier bake survives beyond the new `pageCount` (W45 reports one that does, for instance after a hand copy), and an external edit is never overwritten.
- **Resumption.** An interrupted run (cancel, crash, power loss) leaves every completed unit FRESH and every other unit as it was; running the plan again bakes exactly the remaining units, because freshness is the fingerprint, not a progress file (`Test_WorldBake_InterruptedRunResumes`). A crash mid-batch is recovered by the framework's journal at the next session start.
- **Concurrency with editing.** A unit's snapshot is the pinned plan's content: the saved documents the plan operation read, each recorded with its file digest in the read table, and the plan's summary table; every byte a bake job reads is either a recorded one, checked by the pinned access, or one the operation recorded at its own first open ("World editor extension": "Pinned access"). Unsaved edits are not bake inputs, exactly as play in the editor uses the saved region documents. Editing and saving an input during the bake leaves every unit already written, or still to be written, with the pinned plan's fingerprint, which the next plan finds STALE (`Test_WorldEditor_EditDuringBakeMarksUnitStale`); "Bake stale" then re-bakes exactly those units. One pinned plan per run is chosen over one plan per unit, which would cost one whole-world partition per unit.
- **Geometry the tracer read.** `Zenith_PrepareWorldLightingUnit` decodes each mesh's cooked file through `Zenith_WorldMeshGeometrySource`, which returns, with each mesh's geometry, the LOD 0 geometry digest of exactly the bytes it decoded (computed by GEOM's `Zenith_ComputeMeshGeometryDigest`, row WD30), and recomputes F from the job's P and those digests (`Zenith_FinishWorldLightingFingerprint`). Terrain needs no such step: its tiles are read through the pinned access, so the tracer's terrain is exactly the plan's. The header records that **traced** fingerprint, so it names the geometry traced: when a mesh was edited and re-cooked between the plan and the prepare, the header names the new geometry, and the next plan finds the unit FRESH if the published summary's digest equals it and STALE otherwise; a unit is never written FRESH under one geometry while its traces came from another (`Test_WorldLightBake_TracedFingerprintNamesDecodedGeometry`).
- **Headless runs.** The automation step `world.bake` (registered through `RegisterAutomationStep`, `Zenith2/Docs/Design/EDIT/Panels.md` → "Extension interface") waits for the plan operation's next completed plan, starts the operation with `kinds`, `scope` and `force` arguments, and its poll function returns `EDITOR_OPERATION_IN_FLIGHT` until DONE, CANCELLED or FAILED; the Stage 4 bake of OpenWorldSlice is such a script run by a human (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD.md` → "Stage plan").

## Bake report format

`Games/<Name>/.editor/world/<W>/bake-report.json` (untracked, per user, `Zenith2/Docs/Design/EDIT/Pipeline.md` → "Per-user state stays out of documents"), `$schema` `zenith2.world-bake-report`, version 1, written by `Zenith_WriteWorldBakeReport` with `Zenith_JsonWriter`:

| Member | Content |

## Lighting workflow

The lighting bake of one unit, in three phases with one work-item phase between them (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/PublicAPI.md` → "Lighting bake functions"):

1. **Prepare** (`Zenith_PrepareWorldLightingUnit`, one call, single-threaded): **gather** ("Bake geometry"): receivers, occluders, lights, probes and terrain triangles of the unit's gather rectangle, decoding each mesh and recomputing the traced fingerprint ("Bake execution"); **pack** the receivers into lightmap pages ("Atlas packing"); **build** the BVH over every occluder triangle ("BVH construction"); allocate the unit's output arrays and enumerate its work items.
2. **Trace** (`Zenith_TraceWorldLightingItem`, once per work item, any thread, any order, concurrently for distinct items): lightmap tiles, irradiance-volume slabs and reflection-probe faces ("Tracing").
3. **Finish** (`Zenith_FinishWorldLightingUnit`, one call): fold the items' results into the `Zenith_WorldLightBakeResult`; then **encode** the header and page documents ("Baked lighting documents" in `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Documents.md`).

`Zenith_BakeWorldLightingUnit` is the convenience form running the three phases inline on the calling thread (tests and tools without an editor). The result is what LIGHT's evaluation reads as indirect diffuse (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/GI.md` → "Indirect diffuse selection"): lightmaps and volumes hold **indirect** irradiance plus sky irradiance, never the direct light of the scene's lights, which the renderer computes every frame; reflection probes hold full radiance (direct and indirect) seen from their centre.

### Bake geometry

- **Frame.** Every position is translated into the unit's local frame (world position minus the cell anchor) in `double` and narrowed to `float`, so the tracer's precision is that of a cell, wherever the cell lies.
- **Static mesh instances.** An entity with `Zenith.Render.MeshInstance` (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/RPROD/Extractors.md` → "Render component registration"), property 15 `Visible` true, static ("Grid assignment" in `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Partition.md`: no rigid body on it or an ancestor, or one whose `MotionType` is `PHYSICS_MOTION_TYPE_STATIC`), in a BAKED layer with `bakes.lighting` true. A moving instance (KINEMATIC or DYNAMIC body) is neither occluder nor receiver. Its LOD 0 triangles are read from the mesh's cooked file through GEOM's CPU reader `Zenith_ReadCookedMeshGeometry` (row WD9): positions, normals, UV1 when present, the section of each triangle and the section's material slot. **Occluders** are every such instance whose world bounding sphere ("Mesh summaries") intersects the gather rectangle; **receivers** are the occluders whose hierarchy the partition assigned to the lighting grid, whose world bounding sphere's centre lies in Rc and whose mesh has UV1. A receiver-candidate without UV1, or in a hierarchy assigned to another grid (a landmark promoted to the `large` grid stays visible while the lighting cell unloads, so its lightmap must not be the lighting cell's), is listed in the report's `volumeLit`, lit at run time by the volumes.
- **Albedo.** A triangle's bake albedo is `materialAlbedo[material GUID]` (the instance's override for the section's slot, else the mesh's slot material) when listed, else `defaultAlbedo`; terrain triangles use `terrainAlbedo`. The bake reads no material or texture data: albedo is a declared input of the bake settings, which keeps the bake a function of documents alone.
- **Terrain.** For every terrain of the world and every tile intersecting the gather rectangle, ENV's `Zenith_ExtractNavigationGeometry(document, tile, {}, arena)` triangles over the document `Zenith_WorldLoadTerrain` loaded (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Partition.md` → "Terrain loading"; `Zenith2/Docs/Design/ENV/Bake.md` → "Navigation geometry extraction"), minus hole quads, as occluders. The water argument `axWater` is the empty span: it only marks area codes, and area codes are not bake inputs, so the triangles are the same either way. Terrain is never a receiver (it has no UV1; it is lit at run time by volumes and the sky).
- **Lights.** `Zenith.Lighting.PointLight`, `.SpotLight` and `.DirectionalLight` components of BAKED layers with `bakes.lighting` true and of the persistent scene, whose influence intersects the gather rectangle, converted to candela, lux and radians by WORLD's own code with LIGHT's formulas (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/Lighting.md` → "Light units"; a spot light's `I = Φ / (2π (1 − cos θ_outer))`, a point light's `I = Φ / 4π`), every cosine of a cone angle, the conversion's included, evaluated with CORE's deterministic `Zenith_Cos` (ruling X314-FMA3) and never by LIGHT's `Flux_*` conversion functions, which live in ZenithFlux, a library ZenithAuthoring does not link; plus the settings' `sun`; a world with both a `sun` and a directional light among those components is refused (W37), so no directional light enters the bake twice. Rect and disc lights (Stage 5 types) are not bake inputs at Stage 4; the Stage 5 LIGHT revalidation adds them.
- **Probes.** `Zenith.Lighting.ReflectionProbe` components with the baked capture mode whose entity lies inside Rc and belongs to a hierarchy of a BAKED layer with `bakes.lighting` true that the partition assigned to the lighting grid, so the lighting cell holds the entity whose `BakedAsset` the partition writes (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Partition.md` → "Contributed cell content"). Any other baked-mode probe in Rc (an EDITOR_ONLY or RUNTIME layer's, or one of a hierarchy sent to another grid, a `large` or a layer grid) is not baked and is listed in the report's `probesSkipped`.

### Atlas packing

`Zenith_PackWorldLightmapAtlas` places one square rectangle per receiver:

1. Side `s = clamp(ceil(sqrt(A) × texelsPerMetre), minRectTexels, pageSize − 2 × paddingTexels) + 2 × paddingTexels`, with A the receiver's world-space LOD 0 surface area (triangle areas after the instance's scale), computed in `double`.
2. Sort receivers by (s descending, entity ID ascending).
3. Shelf packing: pages are filled in order; a page holds shelves stacked from y = 0; a shelf's height is its first rectangle's side. A rectangle goes into the first shelf (of the first page, in creation order) with `cursorX + s ≤ pageSize` and `s ≤ shelfHeight`; else into a new shelf at the page's used height of the first page where `usedHeight + s ≤ pageSize`; else onto a new page. More than `uWORLD_MAX_LIGHT_PAGES` pages is `WORLD_ERROR_ATLAS_FULL` naming the unit and the texel density that would fit.
4. The binding of a rectangle at (ox, oy) of page p: `scale = ((s − 2·pad) / pageSize, (s − 2·pad) / pageSize)`, `bias = ((ox + pad) / pageSize, (oy + pad) / pageSize)`, so `uv = UV1 × scale + bias` maps the instance's UV1 square onto the rectangle's interior (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/GI.md` → "Lightmap evaluation").

The packing is a pure function of the sorted receivers; complexity O(n log n + n × shelves).

### BVH construction

A binary BVH over the occluder triangles in the local frame: binned SAH with 12 bins along the axis of largest centroid extent (ties: X before Y before Z), leaves of at most 4 triangles, depth at most 64 (a deeper split becomes a leaf), split cost `1 + (A_L × N_L + A_R × N_R) / A_P` against a leaf cost N; triangles keep their input order inside each partition (a stable partition), and input order is (instance entity ID, triangle index), so the tree is identical across runs. Nodes are 32 bytes (bounds 24, child or first triangle 4, count and axis 4) in one array; triangles are 48-byte records (three vertices, albedo index, instance index). Memory is bounded by the pending row "Lighting bake tracer memory"; a unit over 16,777,216 triangles is `WORLD_ERROR_BAKE_GEOMETRY` naming the unit. The build runs inside the prepare phase on one thread.

### Tracing

All tracing is single-sample path tracing in the local frame with the fixed floating-point environment ([Z2-N-CONTRACT-360]); every sine, cosine and square root is CORE's deterministic function or IEEE `sqrt` (ruling X314-FMA3; row WD29), so a unit's documents are byte-identical on every machine running one build:

- **Ray-triangle test.** Möller–Trumbore in `float`; a hit requires `t > 0`; among equal `t` the lower triangle index wins; traversal visits the nearer child first (ties: the left child).
- **Ray origin.** `p + g × ε`, with g the geometric normal facing the ray's hemisphere and `ε = 0.002 + 1e-6 × max(|p.x|, |p.y|, |p.z|)` metres.
- **Radiance of a ray** `L(o, d, depth)`: no hit → the sky ("Escaped rays"); a hit on a back face (`dot(d, g) > 0`) → 0 and the back-face counter; else `albedo × (E_direct(hit) / π + (depth < bounces − 1 ? L(hit, cosineSample(n), depth + 1) : 0))`.
- **Direct irradiance** `E_direct(x)`: per light, one shadow ray to the light's position (point and spot) or along −direction to infinity (directional, sun); unoccluded: point and spot `I × max(0, dot(n, l)) / d² × window(d)` with LIGHT's range window and spot cone (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/Lighting.md` → "Light attenuation and shape"); directional `E_lux × max(0, dot(n, −dir))`.
- **Lightmap texels.** For each receiver triangle, the texels of its page whose centre lies inside the triangle's UV1 image (edge functions with the top-left rule; a texel claimed by two triangles takes the lower triangle index) get coverage 1, position p, shading normal n (barycentric, normalised) and geometric normal g; `samplesPerTexel` cosine-weighted directions about n; `E = π × mean(L(p, d_i, 0))` per channel, accumulated in `double`. The dominant direction is `normalize(Σ Y(L_i) d_i)` and the directionality `|Σ Y(L_i) d_i| / Σ Y(L_i)` (Y the Rec. 709 luminance), both 0 when `Σ Y = 0`; under a uniform sky with no occluder the directionality is the cosine-weighted mean of `cos θ`, which converges to 2/3. Uncovered texels keep coverage 0; LIGHT's writer dilates them (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/GI.md` → "Bake output contract").
- **Random draws** (the rule the other bullets name "Random draws"). Every draw is `Zenith_RandomSource(ulWORLD_LIGHT_TRACER_SEED, stream)` with the stream `Zenith_Hash64U64(element, Zenith_Hash64U64(target, Zenith_Hash64(unitKey)))` and the counter `sample × 32 + k` before draw k. The pair (target, element) names what is traced, so no two traced elements of a unit share a stream: a lightmap texel of page p has target p (0 … 255) and element its texel index on the page (y × pageSize + x); a volume probe has target `0x10000` and element its index in x-fastest order; a texel of a baked reflection probe has target `0x20000 + j`, j the probe's position in the unit's probes sorted by ascending entity ID, and element `face × 128² + texel` (face 0 … 5, texel y × 128 + x). k = 2 × depth for the first and 2 × depth + 1 for the second uniform of the direction drawn at path depth `depth` (0 for the element's own direction; at most `bounces` ≤ 8, so k ≤ 17 < 32 and no two draws of a sample share a counter); a volume probe's sphere direction is `UnitVector` over the draws k = 0 and 1 (its two `NextUnitDouble` calls, `Zenith2/Docs/Design/CORE/Maths.md` → "Zenith_RandomSource"), and a probe texel's sub-texel jitter uses k = 18 and 19. The seed is the constant `ulWORLD_LIGHT_TRACER_SEED` (`0x576F726C644C6974`, "WorldLit"), not the world's `seed`: noise is a property of the tracer, not of the world, and keying it by a document value would make that value a lighting input ("Lighting fingerprint").
- **Cosine sample.** `(u1, u2)` → `r = sqrt(u1)`, `φ = 2π u2`, local `(r Zenith_Cos(φ), r Zenith_Sin(φ), sqrt(1 − u1))` in the orthonormal basis of n (`Zenith_BuildOrthonormalBasis`, `Zenith2/Docs/Design/CORE/Maths.md` → "Vector types").
- **Irradiance volume.** One volume per unit covering Rc on X and Z and the vertical range of the cell's content (minimum and maximum entity Y, each padded by 2 m, clamped to the world's extent); spacing `volumeSpacing`, dimensions `ceil(extent / spacing) + 1` per axis, each at most 256 and the product at most 1,048,576 (LIGHT's format, `Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/GI.md` → "Irradiance volume cooked format"), the spacing doubled until both hold (reported). Per probe, `volumeSamples` uniform sphere directions (`UnitVector`), `L` traced at depth 0, projected to L1 SH: `c_lm = (4π / M) Σ L_i Y_lm(d_i)` with `Y00 = 0.282095`, `(Y1−1, Y10, Y11) = 0.488603 × (d.y, d.z, d.x)`, then multiplied by `Â_0 = π`, `Â_1 = 2π / 3`, stored as (c00, c1−1, c10, c11) per channel; validity 0 when more than 10% of the rays hit a back face, else 255.
- **Reflection probes.** Per probe, six faces of `uWORLD_PROBE_FACE_SIZE²` (128²) texels in LIGHT's cube layout (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/Reflections.md` → "Reflection probe cooked format"), `probeSamplesPerTexel` jittered primary rays per texel from the capture centre (the jitter inside the texel drawn with k = 18 and 19 of "Random draws", in the stream of that probe and texel); a primary ray's radiance is the full path with direct light at its first hit, `albedo × (E_direct / π + L(next))`, escaped rays the sky; radiance in absolute nits (stored pre-exposure 1); mip 0 only, prefiltered and reduced to the mobile face size by LIGHT's step (row WD25).
- **Work items.** A unit's work items are, in this order: its lightmap pages split into 64 × 64 texel tiles (page-major, then tile rows, then columns), its volume split into slabs of 256 probes (x-fastest probe order), and its probes' faces (probe in ascending entity ID, then face 0 … 5). Item i writes only its own texels, probes or face in the unit's output arrays, so items may run on any thread in any order, concurrently for distinct items, and the folded result is identical for any schedule (`Test_WorldLightBake_WorkerCountInvariant`). An item checks the cancellation flag between its rows of texels; a cancelled item writes nothing further and the unit is discarded.

Complexity per unit: O(T log T) for the BVH over T triangles, plus O(texels × samples × bounces × log T) for the lightmaps, the volume and the probes likewise.

#### Escaped rays

A ray that leaves the scene receives the settings' two-hemisphere sky: radiance `radianceNits × upper` for `d.y > 0`, `radianceNits × lower` for `d.y ≤ 0`. The sky is a declared bake input (part of the fingerprint), not a sample of ENV's atmosphere, so a time-of-day change does not stale every lightmap; the Stage 5 LIGHT revalidation may replace it with the static environment's SH9 (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/LIGHT/Reflections.md` → "Static environment").

## Navigation workflow

1. **Derive.** With navigation enabled, every import derives the navigation input scenes, the per-region navigation documents and AI's products ("Navigation input scenes" in `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Partition.md`); nothing is written to the source tree, so no editor command maintains navigation files and a region create, delete or move touches no navigation data.
2. **Bake.** AI's step `ai.navMesh` bakes each tile product from its `ai.navTileJob`: it reads the cooked navigation input scenes the job names, adds each scene's `offset` in `double` and translates into tile-local coordinates (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/AI/Navigation.md` → "Bake inputs"; row WD23). A collider edit changes one region's navigation input scene and therefore its cooked output's key, which re-bakes exactly the tiles of the derived documents that list that scene: the region's own tiles and those of its eight neighbours (`Zenith2/Docs/Design/AI/Navigation.md` → "Bake determinism").
3. **Stream.** The partition writes each navigation cell's tile entities through AI's contribution ("Contributed cell content" in `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Partition.md`), over the products it declared itself.
4. **Generated content.** Cook-mode and baked generator outputs of navigation-baking layers are in the navigation input scenes like authored content, so a fence placed by a cook-mode generator blocks the navmesh exactly where it stands (`Test_WorldNavigation_CookModeOutputsAreInputs`).

The bake panel's "Bake navigation" sends the cook server a `cook` request for the world's navigation tile products in the selected regions and shows AI's diagnostics; it is a convenience, since every cook bakes stale tiles anyway.

## HLOD workflow

`Zenith_BuildWorldHlodNodes(const Zenith_WorldPartitionPlan&, const Zenith_WorldHlodSettings&, Zenith_Arena&, Zenith_WorldHlodResult&)`, run as step 12 of the partition, for every base grid with `hlod.levels` ≥ 1 when `bakes.hlod.enabled`:

1. **Level-1 nodes.** One node per cell of the grid that holds at least one eligible instance for level 1 ("Instance eligibility"), at the cell's coordinate.
2. **Higher levels.** For k = 2 … levels: one node at `(floor(x / 4), floor(z / 4))` (floor division) for every distinct parent coordinate of the level k − 1 nodes, so every node above level 1 has at least one child and every node below the top has a parent (STREAM's V21, `Zenith2/Docs/Design/STREAM/Formats.md` → "HLOD node records": level factor 4).
3. **Contents.** A level-k node's instances are the level-k-eligible instances of every base cell it covers.
4. **Meshes.** Up to 4 mesh slots per node ("Material modes"); each slot is one `hlod:` product with a merge recipe ("Merge recipes").
5. **Flags.** `castsShadows` is true when any contributing instance has property 13 `CastsShadows` true.

A node whose contents are empty after eligibility is not emitted; a level-1 cell without a node is a counted gap at run time (`stream.hlod.gaps`), which the report lists. Layer grids have `hlod.levels` 0 (W38), so they hold no node and count no gap.

### Instance eligibility

An instance contributes to level k when: it is a static mesh instance (static as "Grid assignment" of `Zenith2/Docs/Design/WORLD/Partition.md` defines it: no rigid body on it or an ancestor, or a STATIC one) of a BAKED layer with `bakes.hlod` true; property 15 `Visible` true and property 14 `HiddenInMain` false; its world bounding sphere's radius ("Mesh summaries") is at least `minInstanceRadius[k − 1]`; and its property 11 `CullDistanceMetres` is 65,535 (RPROD's "no limit", `Zenith2/Docs/Design/RPROD/Extractors.md` → "Render component registration") or at least the node's refine distance (an instance culled before its proxy would show is left out; 0 is a real distance, so an instance with 0, never drawn, is left out).

### Material modes

| Mode | Slots |
|---|---|
| `keep` | the node's instances are grouped by their resolved material per section; groups are sorted by triangle count descending (ties by material GUID) and dealt into up to 4 slots of at most 255 materials each, so each slot keeps the source materials as sections; groups beyond 4 × 255 are dropped with `WORLD_WARNING_HLOD_MATERIALS_DROPPED` |
| `flatten` | one slot, one section: the merged geometry with vertex colour set per source section to the section material's proxy colour, drawn with GEOM's engine HLOD material |

### Merge recipes

A recipe lists, for one slot: the node anchor (the minimum corner of the covered base cells at the grid's base Y, absolute `double`), the triangle target (`trianglesPerNode[k − 1]` split across slots in proportion to their source triangle counts, at least 64 each), `maxError[k − 1]`, the material mode, and per contributing instance in ascending entity ID: the mesh GUID, the source LOD index (0 for level 1; for level k the lowest LOD whose stored error is at most `maxError[k − 1] × node extent / 2`, else the last), the instance transform relative to the node anchor (a 3 × 4 `float` matrix), and the eight material-override GUIDs.
