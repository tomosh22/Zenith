### What the rulings changed about this slice's scope

**1. The appearance palette had ZERO free cells, so the two trainers had nothing
to wear.** `ZM_GetHumanPaletteColour` reads only outfit and hair, giving a 36-cell
space, and against the 0.15 separation floor exactly one cell is free while this
slice needs two. **Ruled (ZM-D-208, ruling 1): append an outfit slot and/or a hair
colour.** Both enums are APPEND-only and save-stable, and appending moves no
existing colour by a single float.

★ **Appending yields CANDIDATE cells, not usable ones.** The new outfit's primary
and accent must be chosen so every resulting blend clears 0.15 against every
existing row AND against the blockout grey (0.52, 0.55, 0.60). **Report the
achieved separations; do not assert them** — an assertion that a colour is far
enough, written by whoever picked the colour, is a self-referential guard and this
codebase has been bitten by that shape three times.

**2. Flagging both trainers retires the last flagless PRODUCTION row, and Ruling
3's own remedy does not reach two of the three consumers.** **Ruled (ZM-D-208,
ruling 2): option (a), extended.** Three parts, all in this slice:

  * Add the by-ROW primitive
    `ZM_ApplyTrainerResultToGameState(ZM_GameState&, const ZM_TrainerData&, ZM_SIDE, bool)`
    and re-point `ZM_Tests_Party.cpp:1099` at it. This is an EXTRACTION: the by-id
    function already resolves the row at `ZM_BattleWriteBack.cpp:133` and works
    purely from it thereafter, so by-id becomes classify + `ZM_IsRegisteredTrainer`
    + resolve + forward.
  * RETIRE phase 8 of `ZM_AutoTests_TrainerSight.cpp`, with the reason recorded IN
    THE TEST FILE: its precondition is a flagless REGISTERED row, which Ruling 3
    makes deliberately unreachable. ★ Do NOT keep a row flagless to preserve it.
  * Do NOT give `ZM_Interactable::ConfigureTrainerSight` a by-row overload — it
    re-derives `m_eTrainerId` from the serialized NPC row by design.

### ★★ THE CLAIM-CHECK REWRITE IS THE SUBTLE PART — DO NOT BUMP THE COUNT

`Npc_ExactlyOneRowNamesARegisteredTrainer` (`Tests/ZM_Tests_NpcData.cpp:885`)
asserts `== 1u` exactly, and a second trainer reds it. **It is not a count check
wearing a count's clothes — it is the tripwire for the
`ZM_TRAINER_RIVAL_VESPER == 0` trap.** That enumerator is FIRST in `ZM_TRAINER_ID`,
so a row that OMITS its trailing initializer value-initialises the column to 0 and
**silently becomes the rival**, with no MSVC warning under warnings-as-errors.

So `== 3u` would keep the file compiling and delete the protection: three rows
still count three whether or not one of them defaulted into Vesper's id. The
rewrite must be **PER-ID** and must make a double-claim RED — an omitted
initializer then shows up as `ZM_TRAINER_RIVAL_VESPER` being claimed twice, which
is exactly the trap, caught by construction rather than by a total.
